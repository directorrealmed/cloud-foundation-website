# Cloud Foundation — Google Ad Grants Playbook

**Goal:** keep the $10,000/month grant permanently, spend as much of it as possible on people who will give or serve, and keep account CTR comfortably above 5%.

**How:** a tightly built account (exact/phrase keywords, two ads per ad group, heavy negatives), real conversion tracking on the site, and a script that checks the account every day and fixes it before Google would.

---

## 1. The rules we must never break (Google Ad Grants policy)

| Rule | How this setup handles it |
|---|---|
| **Account CTR ≥ 5% every month.** Missing it 2 months in a row means suspension. | Built to run at 8–12%. The guard script steers to **6.5%**, not 5%, and trims automatically if CTR drops. |
| No keywords with Quality Score 1–2 | The guard pauses them daily. Keywords with QS "—" are exempt. |
| No single-word keywords (brand terms excepted), and no overly generic ones ("donate", "charity") | The builder refuses to export them, and the guard pauses any that get added later. |
| Each campaign: ≥ 2 ad groups. Each ad group: ≥ 2 ads. | Every ad group ships with two ads. The guard emails you if any campaign or ad group drops below the minimum. |
| ≥ 2 sitelinks, each a different inner page | Six account-level sitelinks, all inner pages. The guard audits this. |
| Relevant geo-targeting | United States, "location of presence". The guard flags any campaign without geo-targeting. |
| Valid conversion tracking, **≥ 1 conversion per month**. Page views and time on site can't be primary conversions. | `js/track.js` (below). The guard emails you if there are 0 conversions in 30 days. |
| Answer the **annual program survey** | Calendar task. Have the survey email go to director@ **and** a second inbox. Missed surveys are a common reason accounts get deactivated. |
| $2 max CPC on manual bidding | We use **Maximize conversions**, which has no $2 cap. That cap is the main reason most grantees fail to spend the full $10k. |

Sources: Google's [account management policy](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/117827), [compliance guide](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402), and [keyword policy](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/7587473). Check them once a quarter; Google changes these rules from time to time.

## 2. What top grantees do (and what we copied)

The accounts that consistently run 8–16% CTR and spend the full grant all do the same few things:

1. **A brand campaign.** Brand searches get 20–40% CTR and pull the account average up.
   - **Watch out:** "Cloud Foundation" is also the name of VMware's (Broadcom's) software product, VMware Cloud Foundation. If we bid on the bare phrase "cloud foundation", IT professionals will see our ad and not click, and our CTR will drop. That is why:
     - the brand campaign uses only qualified terms ("cloud foundation charity", [cloud foundation inc], "fundacion cloud")
     - there is a ~30-term negative list (vmware, vcf, broadcom, vsphere, aws, software…)
2. **One theme per ad group, landing on its own page.** Hunger keywords land on /kitchen, school keywords on /centers, earthquake keywords on /colombia. Relevance is what raises Quality Score and CTR.
3. **Specific, numeric copy.** "$5 buys one hot meal" outperforms "Help make a difference". The site already speaks this way, which is a real advantage.
4. **Phrase and exact match only at launch.** Broad match is where CTR dies. Consider adding it later, only for proven themes and only alongside Smart Bidding.
5. **Smart Bidding on real conversions** (Maximize conversions). This removes the $2 cap and is how large grantees actually spend $10k.
6. **Informational content pages** for "how to help…" searches. This is phase 2 (section 6).
7. **Two ads per ad group, always.** One leads with price, one with story. The weaker one is retired and rewritten every month.

- **Totals:** 84 keywords (phrase or exact), 26 responsive search ads (each with 14–15 headlines and 4 descriptions), 6 sitelinks, 6 callouts, and 3 shared negative lists.
- **Budget:** every campaign gets $329/day, the whole daily grant. Google caps total grant spend at about $329/day, so no single campaign is ever starved.

- **Network:** Google Search only.

**To import:** open Google Ads Editor, download the account, then go to Account → Import → From file. Import in this order:

1. `1-campaigns.csv`
2. `2-keywords.csv`
3. `3-negative-keyword-lists.csv` — import as **shared negative lists** and apply all three to every campaign
4. `4-responsive-search-ads.csv`
5. `5-sitelinks-account.csv` and `6-callouts-account.csv` — **account level**

Then review the changes and post them.

**To change copy or keywords:** edit `build_campaigns.py`, run `python3 ads/build/build_campaigns.py`, and re-import. The builder fails if anything breaks a character limit or an Ad Grants rule.

## 4. The self-checking, self-correcting system

The account is checked at four levels:

```
 DAILY  (automatic) grant-guardian.js
   ├─ measure month-to-date CTR and project month end
   ├─ enforce: pause QS 1–2 keywords and single-word keywords
   ├─ protect: pause keywords under 3% CTR after 200 impressions
   │           add [exact] negatives for search terms shown 40+ times with <1.5% CTR
   │           pause an ad running at <60% of its sibling's CTR (always keeps 2 or more)
   ├─ EMERGENCY (CTR < 6.5% from day 4): pause the worst keywords until the projection is back above 6.5%
   ├─ audit: ad groups / ads / sitelinks / geo / conversions / spend pace
   └─ log to a Google Sheet + email "SAFE / WATCH / AT RISK"

 WEEKLY (15 min, human) — read the email, then:
   ├─ search terms report: promote converting terms to keywords
   ├─ un-pause anything the guard paused by mistake, and label it "AG Protect"
   └─ add negatives the script can't infer (themes, not single terms)

 MONTHLY (1 hr, human) — on the 1st:
   ├─ replace every ad the guard paused (copy the winner's angle, change one variable)
   ├─ add 1 new ad group for the best-converting theme
   └─ check the Ad Grants dashboard for policy notices

 YEARLY — complete the program survey and confirm Google for Nonprofits eligibility.
```

**Setting up the guard:**

1. In Google Ads, go to Tools → Bulk actions → Scripts → **+**. Paste `ads/scripts/grant-guardian.js`, then Authorize.
2. Run it with `DRY_RUN: true`. Read the log and confirm every proposed change makes sense.
3. Create a blank Google Sheet and put its URL in `SPREADSHEET_URL`. Set `DRY_RUN: false` and schedule it **Daily, 5 am**.
4. Add **"AG Protect"** to any keyword or ad group the guard must never touch. For example, a strategic keyword with low CTR that brings big donors.
5. Belt and braces: also create Google's recommended **automated rule**. Pause keywords → all enabled keywords → Quality Score < 3 → daily at 11 pm.

**Why the account should not fall below 5% for two months running:**

- The guard steers to 6.5%, which leaves 1.5 points of headroom.
- It acts within 24 hours, and from day 4 of the month it trims hard.
- A month that does dip below 5% triggers "AT RISK" emails every day, which gives you a full second month to recover before suspension.

The trims cost reach, not compliance. Losing some impressions is always cheaper than losing $120,000 a year.

## 5. Conversions (required: at least 1 per month)

`js/track.js` is now loaded on every page. It does nothing until three IDs are filled in at the top of the file:

| Event | Fires when | Set in Google Ads as |
|---|---|---|
| `donate_click` | Click on the Zeffy card form, or copying the Zelle address | **Primary** |
| `generate_lead` | Contact or volunteer form sent (visitor lands on `/thanks/`) | **Primary** |
| `contact_click` | mailto: / tel: link clicked | Secondary |
| `view_give_page` | Reached /give | Secondary (never primary: page views are not allowed as primary conversions) |

**Setup:**

1. Create a GA4 property and paste its `G-…` ID into `GA4_ID`.
2. Link GA4 to Google Ads, then import `donate_click` and `generate_lead` as conversions.
3. Optional: also create a Google Ads conversion called "Donation intent" and paste its `AW-…` ID and label into `ADS_ID` and `ADS_DONATE_LABEL`.

**Upgrade when you can:** track a *completed* Zeffy donation, not just the click to it. If Zeffy lets you set a redirect URL after payment, point it to `https://cloudfoundation.us/thanks/?d=1`. The site then fires `donation_complete`; import it as the top primary conversion. Smart Bidding learns much faster from real gifts.

## 6. Website: what was fixed, and the roadmap to "top-nonprofit" level

**Fixed in this change:**

- **Contact and volunteer forms were not working.** They were missing Netlify's form attribute, so submissions were lost. They now post to Netlify with a honeypot and land on a new `/thanks/` page, which is the conversion.
- **Canonical tags pointed to `/about.html` and similar.** Those URLs didn't exist, and the catch-all rewrite served the *home page* for them, which Google treats as a soft 404. Canonicals are fixed, old `.html` URLs now 301-redirect to the real pages, and unknown URLs get a real 404 page.
- **Phone link:** the displayed number was (786) 205-8800, but the link dialed a 954 number.
- **Privacy page:** it loaded a stylesheet that doesn't exist, so it rendered unstyled. It now uses the site design and discloses Google Analytics/Ads (Google requires this).
- **New:** `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, NGO structured data (EIN, address) on the home page, and security headers.

**Roadmap, in order of impact on conversions and Quality Score:**

1. **A card donation form for every programme.** Today only the Colombia earthquake response has one (Zeffy). Kitchen and Transformation Center visitors can only give by Zelle or cheque, which loses most ad traffic. Create Zeffy forms for *Street Kitchen*, *Transformation Centers* and *Where needed most* (Zeffy is fee-free), and put the button on each programme page above the fold. **This is the single biggest lever.**
2. **Monthly giving** ("$25/month feeds 5 people every month"). Top charities (charity: water's *The Spring*, Compassion's sponsorship) build around recurring gifts.
3. **A dedicated `/ministry/` page** for church planting, prison ministry and leadership training. Campaign 06 lands on `/about/` today; a matching page will raise its Quality Score. After that, add an `/anti-trafficking/` page, written carefully for confidentiality.
4. **Spanish pages** (`/es/…`) for Spanish-speaking donors in Miami and the US generally. Then add Spanish campaigns. Ads must land on pages in the same language.
5. **Proof of impact on each page:** a running counter (meals served this year), a recent dated photo update, and one named story with consent. This is what charity: water, Feeding America and Compassion lead with.
6. **Content pages for informational searches**, e.g. "How to help Colombia after the earthquake", "Hunger in Cali: the facts", "What it costs to build a rural school". These earn cheap, high-CTR clicks and newsletter sign-ups.
7. **Email capture** ("Get one photo update a month"), counted as a *secondary* conversion.
8. **Performance and accessibility:** compress the `img/` photos (several are larger than they need to be), and check that each photo's alt text matches what it shows. Some file names and alt texts appear to have been swapped in earlier commits.
9. **Google Business Profile** for the Hialeah office. Ad Grants accounts can now use Performance Max on Search and Maps placements, which needs a profile.

## 8. Numbers to watch

- **Account CTR (month):** target ≥ 8%; the guard acts below 6.5%; the policy minimum is 5%.
- **Spend:** month 1 is typically $1–3k, rising to $6–10k by month 3 once conversions feed Smart Bidding.
- **Conversions:** ≥ 1/month is required; aim for 30+ per month so Smart Bidding has enough to learn from.
- **Conversion rate:** if it goes above about 15%, check that the tracking isn't double-firing. Agencies report that Google reviews unusually high rates.
- **Quality Score:** the average should be 6 or higher; anything at 3 or below is a candidate to rewrite or remove.
